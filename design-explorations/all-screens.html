Design exploration

Recyclens — four directions
for every screen

Scan, Result and History drawn four ways, on the real Yogurt tub and Coffee cup fixtures from src/vision/mock.ts and the real rule rule-pp from src/rules/pune.json. All four wear the same brand — the Apple-inspired tokens already in src/global.css. What changes is structure: what leads, what is promoted, and how much reasoning is on screen at once.

Variants 4
A

Grouped Inset

Every screen is the iOS grouped-inset list — white cards on grey, uppercase section headers, chevron rows. Optimizes for instant familiarity and fast scanning; nothing here has to be learned. · Tradeoff the verdict reads as one row among many, so the answer loses its weight.

Variant A · Scan, Result, History

What works

Nothing has to be learned — this is the list idiom every phone user already reads without thinking, so the six attributes scan at a glance as label/value pairs. It is also the cheapest to build and the hardest to get wrong: no custom chrome, and one new primitive (HistoryRow) rather than four.

What risks

The verdict is the product, and here it sits in a card that looks like every other card — "Recyclable" at 28px inside an inset group reads as a setting, not an answer. It also leans hardest into Cross-Platform Costume: iOS Settings rendered on Android is the most recognisable tell of the four.

Pick this if

You want the lowest-risk build and a demo nobody can misread, and you accept the verdict sharing visual weight with its evidence.

B

Answer First

The verdict owns the first screenful as a tinted hero; all six reads and the rule collapse behind one Why this answer row. The camera goes full-bleed with glass controls over it. Optimizes for the answer landing in under a second, and a camera that feels like a camera. · Tradeoff the reasoning — the product's entire claim — is one tap away instead of on screen.

Variant B · Scan, Result, History
Manifest The 1 rule, 6 reads counter is honest for a fresh scan but not for a reopened one — StoredAttributes is Partial, so an older scan can carry fewer than six. The disclosure row has to count what is actually there, not print "6".

What works

Someone holding a yogurt tub over a bin gets their answer without reading anything else, and the tinted hero gives each verdict its own unmistakable colour field while still carrying icon and word. The full-bleed camera is also the only variant where the viewfinder looks like a real camera app rather than a photo in a card.

What risks

Recyclens exists because it shows its reasoning; folding the rule, its citation and all six reads behind one row concedes exactly that. In a four-person project graded on a defensible ML component, a demo where the evidence never appears on screen is the weakest possible framing of your own work.

Pick this if

You think the real user is mid-bin-decision and will almost never open the reasoning, and you are willing to defend that in the write-up.

C

Evidence Record

The result reads as an audit record: verdict as a compact band, the cited rule promoted directly under it, then all six reads as a dense evidence table with the deciding row highlighted. Provenance is visible from the Scan screen onward. Optimizes for defensibility — every claim on screen shows where it came from. · Tradeoff the densest of the four; the answer competes with its own evidence.

Variant C · Scan, Result, History
Manifest The history row's second line (rule-pp · National) is aspirational: listScans selects six columns and never parses a stored attribute payload (spec 0006, AC-8). Showing it means widening ScanSummary — a real cost this direction has to pay or drop.

What works

This is the only direction that argues the product's thesis on screen: verdict, then the named rule, then the cited document with its authority chip, then the evidence with the deciding read called out. For a graded project it is also the most demonstrable — a marker can trace any answer back to a source without tapping anything.

What risks

Density is the cost: the verdict band is compact enough that on a bright phone outdoors it can lose to the table under it, and the 11px uppercase captions sit below the four-size type scale, so either the scale gains a fifth size or those captions grow and the table gets taller.

Pick this if

The reasoning is the product and the submission is graded on it — and you would rather defend density than defend a hidden rule.

D

Sheet over Photo

Apple's Visual Look Up idiom: the shot you just took stays on screen and the answer rides up over it on a sheet, with a segmented control switching between Answer, Rule and Reads. History becomes a photo grid. Optimizes for unbroken continuity from shutter to answer — you never lose sight of what you photographed. · Tradeoff a hand-rolled sheet plus a photo-dependent history, in a codebase that already flags hand-rolled chrome as a known debt.

Variant D · Scan, Result, History
Manifest The photo grid is ⚠️ Sometimes data. src/db/index.ts warns that photoUri can point at a file that is no longer there, and photo.tsx already ships a "That photo is gone" state — a grid built on photos needs a per-tile fallback that a text list would not.

What works

Continuity: the thing you photographed never leaves the screen, so "Recyclable" is visibly attached to that tub rather than to an abstract record. The segmented control is also the only structure here that fits the answer, the rule and all six reads on one screen with no scrolling and no hidden disclosure.

What risks

It is the most build, by a distance — a draggable sheet, a segmented control and a grid, none of which exist in src/components/ today, on a project where the Scan screen's own camera chain is still unproven on a device. The segmented control also hides two of three panels, which is the same concession variant B makes, just in smaller print.

Pick this if

The demo is the deliverable and you have time to spend on motion — this is the one that will look best in a two-minute video.

=

The same in all four

Two surfaces are not a choice between directions, so they are drawn once. The Photo modal is fixed by spec 0007 AC-10: the photo is judged against ink, not against a white app surface, because a white surround throws a colour cast onto the thing you are trying to look at. The state screens all come from one StateView — icon, title, explanation, up to two actions — and whichever direction wins inherits them unchanged.

Shared · Photo modal, permission state, failure state

Already decided

The photo-on-ink treatment, the five storage-failure messages and the camera-permission copy are settled in spec 0007 and in src/screens/result/index.tsx. None of the four directions touches them.

Still open

The two dev-* routes are excluded here — features 8 and 11 delete them. If either survives to the demo it needs a treatment, and none of these four give it one.

Inherited either way

Whichever direction wins, StateView, AppButton, IconTile and verdict.ts carry over. The choice is layout, not primitives.

How to pick a direction

Pick on the tradeoff you are willing to defend, not on which screenshot looks best.

If you want → A The cheapest, least-risky build, and you accept that the verdict shares weight with everything around it.
If you want → B The answer to land in under a second for someone standing over a bin, with the reasoning one tap behind it.
If you want → C The reasoning argued on screen — verdict, named rule, cited source, deciding read. The defensible one.
If you want → D The best two-minute demo, and you have the time to build a sheet, a segmented control and a grid.
A reading, not a vote Recyclens is a four-person graded project whose stated claim is that you can read its reasoning and trust it. C is the direction that argues that claim, and its two real costs — density, and an 11px caption that the four-size scale does not have — are both fixable: promote the captions to text-label and let the table grow. B is the strongest if you decide the claim is marketing and the real use is bin-side. A and D are the honest poles: A is what you build if time runs out, D is what you build if it does not.
